Tell me about yourself
I'm Adama Diallo, a computer science student and aspiring software developer from Dakar, Senegal. For me, software is more than a technical discipline: it's a way to turn an idea into something practical that people can use.
How do you approach your work?
I try to stay curious and disciplined, and to understand the real need behind a project before writing code. I want to know how things work, not just make them run, and keep improving instead of settling for a superficial result.
What are your objectives?
I want to become a versatile, highly skilled software engineer with a portfolio of useful, reliable and well-designed applications. I'm looking for real experience through internships and collaboration, and I'd like to study and work abroad, with France, Spain and the United States as possible destinations.
How do your projects help people?
I want them to address real needs: simplify tasks, improve access to services or information, and make daily life easier. To me, software is a tool for solving problems and creating value, not just code.
What motivates you beyond coding?
Becoming independent, building a meaningful career, and being able to support the people I care about. I want to be someone others can rely on, and to keep growing as a person along the way.
